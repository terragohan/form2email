{% extends "guide-base.html" %} {% block heading %}A newsletter signup that emails you each subscriber{% endblock %} {% block standfirst %}The one-field subscribe box in your blog's footer, sidebar, or at the end of every post — each new subscriber arrives in your inbox.{% endblock %} {% block about %}

Readers who finish a post are the ones most likely to want the next one. A small subscribe box right where the content ends — or always present in the footer or sidebar — catches them at that moment, and one email field is all it takes.

If your blog is static — plain HTML on any host — it can't receive the form POST itself. Form2Email receives it and emails you the address, and you add it to whatever tool actually sends your newsletter.

{% endblock %} {% block example %} <form action="{{ settings.base_url.rstrip('/') }}/f/your-token" method="post"> <label for="email">Get new posts by email</label> <input id="email" name="email" type="email" required> <!-- optional: your own thank-you page after submit --> <input type="hidden" name="_redirect" value="https://yoursite.com/thanks"> <button type="submit">Subscribe</button> </form> {% endblock %} {% block example_notes %}

That's the whole integration — small enough to sit in a sidebar or footer partial. Only the action, method="post", and the input name attributes matter to Form2Email; style it to match your site.

{% endblock %} {% block mapping %}

Each submission becomes one email to your destination address:

  • Reply-To is the subscriber's email, so you can reply to them directly.
  • The address also appears in the body, ready to copy into your newsletter tool.
  • Any other fields you add (a first name, a topic checkbox, anything) are listed as additional fields.

With _redirect set, the subscriber's browser is sent to your thank-you page after a successful submit — a 303 redirect. Without it they get a small JSON response — fine for JavaScript-driven forms, less friendly for plain HTML.

{% endblock %} {% block tips %}
  • Keep it to one field. Email only — every extra field costs you subscribers, and you can ask for a name later.
  • Put the box where readers finish content: the end of each post, the footer, or the sidebar — not a popup the moment they arrive.
  • Make the button say what happens: "Subscribe" or "Get new posts by email", not "Submit".
  • Use _redirect to send subscribers to a thank-you page that tells them what to expect and how often you write.
  • Add {{ settings.mail_from }} to your contacts so the verification and signup emails don't land in spam.
{% endblock %} {% block limitations %}
  • Form2Email is not a newsletter service: no double opt-in, no list management, no sending campaigns. You get one email per signup and add the address to your newsletter tool yourself — check that tool's consent requirements before importing.
  • Subscribers get no confirmation email — Form2Email only ever emails you, the endpoint owner. Your thank-you page is where you set expectations.
  • The endpoint expires {{ settings.default_ttl_days }} days after verification — extend it for free from your manage page as often as you like (extending also revives an expired endpoint), or ask the operator for a permanent endpoint.
  • Submissions are accepted only from the exact domain you registered. If your blog answers on both yoursite.com and www.yoursite.com, register the one your form is served from (or create one endpoint per hostname).
  • Spam protection is origin checks plus per-IP rate limits (30 submissions a minute, with exponential backoff) — there is no CAPTCHA or honeypot, so skim the addresses before you import them.
