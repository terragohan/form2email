{% extends "guide-base.html" %} {% block heading %}Appointment requests that arrive by email{% endblock %} {% block standfirst %}Let visitors ask for a service, a date, and a time without phone tag — the request lands in your inbox and you confirm it with a reply.{% endblock %} {% block about %}

If you run a salon, a coaching practice, a photography studio, or a small clinic, most bookings start the same way: someone picks a service and a time that suits them, and you say yes or suggest another slot. A form handles that first step better than a phone number or a bare email address on the page — the visitor writes it when it suits them, and every request arrives in the same shape.

One honest note up front: this is a booking request form, not a scheduler. Form2Email delivers the request to your inbox; checking your calendar and confirming the appointment is your job, done by hitting reply.

Swap the options for your actual services and style the fields however you like — only the action, method="post", and the input name attributes matter to Form2Email. The form works with or without JavaScript, on any host that can serve an HTML page.

{% endblock %} {% block mapping %}

Each request becomes one email to your destination address:

  • Reply-To is the visitor's email, so confirming — or suggesting another slot — is one reply away.
  • name appears in the body.
  • service, date, time, and notes are listed as additional fields. The date and time inputs arrive as plain text like 2026-10-24 and 14:30, exactly as the browser sent them.

With _redirect set, the visitor's browser is sent to your thank-you page after a successful submit — a good place to say "I'll confirm by email within a day". Without it they get a small JSON response, which is fine for JavaScript-driven forms but less friendly for plain HTML.

{% endblock %} {% block tips %}
  • Use a <select> for the service. A dropdown of your real services keeps replies short and consistent, and spares visitors a free-text "what do you want?" field.
  • Ask for one preferred date and time, plus an alternative in the notes. Most requests then resolve in a single reply either way.
  • Confirm fast. The visitor's address is already the Reply-To, and a same-day answer is usually what turns a request into a booking.
  • State your opening hours or typical availability in plain text next to the form — it heads off most requests for slots you can't take.
  • Add {{ settings.mail_from }} to your contacts so the verification and request emails don't land in spam.
{% endblock %} {% block limitations %}
  • This is a booking request, not a scheduler: no calendar sync, no availability checking. The date and time arrive as plain text, and checking them against your actual calendar is up to you.
  • The visitor gets no automatic confirmation email — Form2Email only ever emails you, the owner. The appointment is confirmed when you reply.
  • The endpoint expires {{ settings.default_ttl_days }} days after verification. Extend it for free from your manage page — extending also revives an expired endpoint — or ask the operator for a permanent endpoint.
  • Submissions are accepted only from the exact domain you registered. If your site answers on both yoursite.com and www.yoursite.com, register the one your form is served from (or create one endpoint per hostname).
